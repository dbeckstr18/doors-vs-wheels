01. A fixed starting point

The reference case begins at 78.5 billion doors and 98 billion wheels on 19 October 2026 at 00:00 UTC. These are editorial estimates informed by the research below. The displayed total is calculated directly from your device’s clock, so reloading does not reset it.

02. Production is not growth

New products often replace old ones. The model subtracts an explicit physical-loss allowance. Hot Wheels and road-vehicle production have recent anchors; most other manufacture rates and every destruction rate are assumptions. Gross tire sales are never added as extra wheels.

03. An honest time horizon

The clock projects linearly for one year after the baseline. It stops when that horizon expires and asks for a research update. It does not collect new statistics automatically. “Pause” freezes the display; resuming catches up to the current model time.

04. Uncertainty comes first

Starting-stock ranges are judgmental stress tests. To widen them over time, low scenarios use half of gross manufacture and twice the destruction; high scenarios use 1.5× manufacture and half the destruction. These are not probabilities, statistical error bars, or independently sampled variables.

What counts—and where the model is incomplete

Counted

Movable doors that open passages or compartments; functional wheels on vehicles, furniture, toys and small guide mechanisms; steering wheels; intact spare and stored components. Two wheel discs on a twin caster count as two. A tire plus its rim counts once. A door on wheels contributes to both categories.

Outside this convention

Ordinary lids and caps, windows, drawer fronts, decorative molded doors, painted wheels, gears, pulleys, sprockets and ball bearings. Long conveyor rollers are excluded. Toy doors must actually open.

Known gaps

There is no comprehensive global inventory of cabinet doors, chairs, luggage wheels or intact discarded components. In-use statistics are imperfect proxies for extant stock. The miscellaneous allowances do not solve landfill coverage. Household averages are not universally representative.

A revised starting estimate

The earlier exploratory wheel total was 89 billion. This version adds 4 billion Hot Wheels wheels after newer cumulative-production reporting, and 5 billion construction-toy wheels after a later LEGO output series. Both revisions still depend on unmeasured survival. The door baseline remains 78.5 billion.

THE EVIDENCE, WITH ITS LIMITS

Show your sources.

A source can establish the scale of an industry without establishing today’s surviving stock. These notes explain exactly what each reference contributes.

Research approach and rejected shortcuts

Reviewed institutional demographic and building-stock research; industry vehicle statistics; appliance assessments; toy-company cumulative and annual output; selected chair, caster and luggage disclosures; and waste/reuse evidence. Priority went to primary reports and attributable company statements. Company promotional figures are identified as such.

Excluded dollar-market forecasts converted into unit counts without a defensible unit price, unsupported global office-chair and luggage totals, double-counting imported/exported products, adding supplier caster output to products already counted, treating tire replacements as new wheels, and treating reuse or service retirement as physical destruction.

Research reviewed on 19 October 2026. Source observation years differ. The review found no representative global cabinet-door average or credible worldwide component-destruction series. The counter is consequently a scenario-based educational estimate, not a real-time census.
